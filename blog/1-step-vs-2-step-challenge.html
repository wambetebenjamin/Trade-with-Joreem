1 Step vs 2 Step challenge: which funded account actually fits you?

One phase with a big drawdown, or two phases with the cheapest entry price on the market? The honest answer depends less on the target and more on how you lose.

Key takeaways

  • 1 Step Flex: one phase, 10% target, 12% static drawdown, no consistency rule, from $49.
  • 2 Step Standard: two phases (8% then 5%), 10% static drawdown, 40% best-day guideline, from $39.
  • Choose Flex if your edge produces occasional large winning days; choose 2 Step if you grind small and consistent.
  • Zero Challenge skips the evaluation entirely — lower drawdown, weekly rewards, 80% split.

Every dollar of difference between these programs comes from one question: how much room do you need to be wrong? A trader who takes 12 trades a day with a tight stop needs less patience than a trader who holds one swing position for a week — but needs more capital cushion per session. The real difference is the consistency rule

Targets are easy to compare mathematically; the consistency rule is where traders get surprised. On 2 Step, if your best day is more than 40% of total profit when a phase is verified, the phase is not failed — it simply is not clear yet, and you keep trading until the ratio normalises.

That sounds harmless until your first day produces $6,000 of an $8,000 target. Now you need another $7,000 of profit before the best-day ratio drops under 40%, which turns a one-week pass into a three-week grind. Flex has no such rule. Hit 10% and you are funded.

Worked example — the consistency trap

2 Step 100K, day 1: +$6,200 (a great gold day). Target is $8,000, so you need $1,800 more — but $6,200 can only be 40% of total profit, which means total profit must reach $15,500. You now need another $9,300: more than the original target. Cap your daily wins at 2–3% and this never happens.

Which one should you buy?

Choose 1 Step Flex if…

  • Your strategy has occasional high-conviction days with outsized wins.
  • You trade news, breakouts or volatility expansions where results cluster.
  • You want the widest drawdown on the market relative to price (12% static).
  • You are impatient with process and want the shortest possible path to funded.

Choose 2 Step Standard if…

  • You are newer and want the cheapest possible attempt ($39 for a 5K account).
  • You make steady, small daily gains rather than spike days.
  • You want a two-phase structure that forces discipline before real size.
  • You plan to scale to $400K later — the scaling path is identical on either program.

Choose Zero Challenge if…

  • You already have a proven, funded-account-grade process.
  • You want weekly rewards instead of bi-weekly cash flow.
  • You are comfortable with a tighter 6% maximum loss in exchange for skipping the exam.

The decision rule I actually use

Look at your last 60 trading days. Find your single best day as a percentage of total profit. If it is above 40%, buy Flex or Zero. If it is below 40%, buy 2 Step and save the money.

That one statistic predicts more evaluation outcomes than any indicator on your chart. If you do not have 60 days of data yet, that is the answer to a different question: you need a process before you need an evaluation — the mentorship and the bookstore exist for exactly that stage.

Cost per attempt, and cost per pass

The sticker price is not the real cost. If a $499 2 Step attempt takes you two tries, your real cost is $998 — which is more than the Flex evaluation at $649, if Flex passes on the first attempt. Compare programs on the cost of the path, not the cost of the first step, and use the reset discount rather than paying full price twice.

Comparison FAQ

Is a 1 step challenge harder than 2 step?

It is a different exam, not a harder one. A 1 step challenge asks for 10% in one phase with a larger drawdown and no consistency rule; a 2 step asks for 8% then 5% with tighter rules but the ability to reset your approach between phases.

Can I switch programs after buying?

No — a purchased evaluation runs on the rules of the program you bought. You can buy a second account on a different program and run them in parallel up to your $400K combined allocation.

Do both programs scale to $400K?

Yes. The scaling plan is identical: hit the quarterly criteria and your allocation grows, with the split climbing to 95% at the top of the plan.

Which program has the fastest payout?

Zero Challenge pays weekly, which is the fastest cycle we offer. 2 Step and Flex pay bi-weekly. All payouts run through M-Pesa, Airtel Money, MTN MoMo, bank transfer, Pesapal, PayPal or crypto.
