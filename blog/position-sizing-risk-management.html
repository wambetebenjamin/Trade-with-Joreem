Position sizing formula: how to risk 1% correctly

Two traders can take the same trade, at the same level, with the same stop — and only one of them will survive the month. The difference is size. Here is the arithmetic, worked out properly.

Key takeaways

  • Position size is derived from three inputs: account equity, risk percentage and stop distance.
  • Formula for FX: lots = (equity × risk%) ÷ (stop pips × pip value per lot).
  • Fixed-dollar risk beats fixed lots because it self-adjusts as your account and volatility change.
  • Recalculate after every change to your balance — including after a reward payout.
  • Never widen a stop to keep a size. Change the size to keep the stop.

Position sizing is the least glamorous skill in trading and the single highest-value one. It is also the skill most often skipped, because every social-media post shows the winner and none show the size relative to the account. If you get sizing right, an average strategy survives long enough to become a good one. If you get it wrong, a good strategy can still kill the account.

The formula

Three inputs, one output:

Risk amount = account equity × risk percentage
Position size = risk amount ÷ (stop distance × value per unit)

For forex pairs quoted in USD (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, AUD/USD), one standard lot moves approximately $10 per pip. So for a 100K account risking 1% with a 30-pip stop:

  • Risk amount = $100,000 × 1% = $1,000
  • Stop distance = 30 pips
  • Lots = $1,000 ÷ (30 × $10) = 0.33 lots (round down to 0.33)

If you had simply traded 1 lot — a very common default — the same stop would cost $300, or 0.3% of the account. Do that three times in a day and you have used 90% of a 1% daily budget. That is how accounts die quietly.

Why fixed-dollar risk beats fixed lots

If you always trade the same lot size, your risk in dollars changes every time the market's volatility does. A 15-pip stop on a quiet Asian session and a 70-pip stop after a CPI release are not remotely the same trade — but with fixed lots they cost you wildly different amounts. Fixed-dollar risk normalises that: volatile conditions automatically buy you a smaller size.

It also compounds the right way. As your account grows, your risk grows proportionally and your P&L scales. After a drawdown, size shrinks automatically, which protects the recovery.

Gold, indices and crypto: adjust the per-unit value

The formula does not change — only the value of one pip per lot does. Common approximations for a standard lot:

  • XAU/USD (gold): 1 lot = 100 oz, so a $1.00 move = $100. A 5-dollar stop on 1 lot risks $500.
  • US30 / NAS100: contract value varies; check the platform's specification window.
  • BTC/USD: 1 lot usually equals 1 BTC, so a $500 stop on 1 lot risks $500.

On MT5, open the specification window and confirm "tick value". Two minutes of checking prevents the most expensive category of mistake on a funded account.

Sizing against the drawdown rules, not just the trade

On an evaluation, size has a second constraint: the daily loss limit. If you risk 1% per trade on a 5% daily limit, you can lose five trades before the day ends. That is a comfortable margin. If you risk 2.5% per trade, two losses ends your day — and one of those losses will inevitably occur on a day when you have already taken a loss.

The four sizing errors that end funded accounts

  1. Widening the stop to keep the lot size. If the structure needs a wider stop, the size must shrink. Always.
  2. Revenge sizing. Doubling after a loss to "get it back" turns a 1% loss into a 2% loss and then a 4% loss.
  3. Sizing off account size instead of equity. After a $6,000 drawdown, a 1% risk is 1% of $94,000, not of the original $100,000.
  4. Forgetting correlated positions. Long EUR/USD, GBP/USD and AUD/USD is one dollar trade, not three 1% risks — it is a 3% risk.

A one-page process you can actually follow

  1. Mark the level. Define the invalidation before entry.
  2. Measure the stop distance in pips or dollars.
  3. Open a calculator and solve for size at 1% or less.
  4. Round down to the nearest micro lot.
  5. Check total open risk including correlated pairs — keep the portfolio under 3%.
  6. Stack your account with a funded account when risk is a habit, not a calculation.

Position sizing FAQ

Should I risk a fixed percentage or a fixed dollar amount?

A fixed percentage of equity. It scales your size up as the account grows and down after a drawdown, which keeps your risk constant relative to the capital you actually have.

What lot size is 1% risk on a 50K account?

At a 40-pip stop on a USD-quoted major, 1% of a 50K account is $500, which equals 1.25 standard lots. With a 20-pip stop it is 2.5 lots; with an 80-pip stop it is 0.62 lots.

Is 1% too small to make money?

No. A 1% risk trader making 2R per week on three trades compounds to roughly 6% a month — that is $6,000 on a 100K funded account, or $5,400 net at a 90% split.

How do I size when trading gold or crypto?

Same formula, different per-unit value. Check the platform specification window for tick value, then solve for size. On gold, a $5 stop on one standard lot risks $500.
